# PokeIQ Vision

## What PokeIQ Is

PokeIQ is a personalized intelligence platform for Pokémon players.

Its purpose is not simply to display information that a player could find elsewhere.

Its purpose is to understand:

- What the player owns
- What the player is trying to accomplish
- What resources the player has available
- What is happening in the game right now
- What is coming in the future
- What opportunities exist around the player
- Which decisions create the most value for that specific player

And then answer the most important question:

> Given everything PokeIQ knows about me, what should I do next?

PokeIQ should eventually feel less like a database or collection of tools and more like a knowledgeable Pokémon assistant that understands the player's account.

---

# The Core Idea

Most Pokémon companion tools answer isolated questions.

PokeIQ should connect those questions together.

Instead of only saying:

> A Mamoswine spawned nearby.

PokeIQ should be able to say:

> A Mamoswine spawned nearby. Based on your current collection, it could improve your Ground raid team by approximately 5%.

Instead of only saying:

> These Pokémon are part of Generation IV.

PokeIQ should understand that the player currently has research requiring Generation IV catches and say:

> There is a cluster of Generation IV Pokémon nearby that could help complete your current research.

Instead of only saying:

> You have 120 Rare Candy.

PokeIQ should eventually be able to say:

> You could spend 80 Rare Candy on this Pokémon now, but a confirmed upcoming raid rotation contains a much higher-value target for your account. Saving is probably the better option.

The information itself is useful.

The connection between that information and the player's situation is what makes it PokeIQ.

---

# Personalization Is the Foundation

PokeIQ should become increasingly personalized as it learns more about the player's Pokémon account.

Two players looking at the same raid boss, spawn map, event, or Pokémon should not necessarily receive the same advice.

One player may desperately need a Ground attacker.

Another may already have an elite Ground team.

One player may need a Pokémon for research.

Another may need it for a Living Dex.

Another may simply want the shiny.

PokeIQ should understand those differences.

The long-term goal is for recommendations to be based on the player's actual:

- Pokémon collection
- Raid teams
- Resources
- Projects and goals
- Research tasks
- Collections
- Play priorities
- Upcoming opportunities
- Current game environment

The more PokeIQ understands, the more useful its advice should become.

---

# PokeIQ Should Help Players Decide

A guiding principle for future development should be:

> Does this feature help the player make a better decision?

PokeIQ does not need to duplicate every Pokémon website, database, calculator, or companion application.

Information should be included when PokeIQ can do something useful with it.

For example:

A list of current raid bosses is useful.

But:

> Giratina is currently available and would immediately become the strongest member of your Ghost team.

is much more valuable.

An event calendar is useful.

But:

> This event begins Friday and contains three Pokémon relevant to your current Projects.

is PokeIQ.

A spawn map is useful.

But:

> There are four Pokémon worth catching within ten minutes of you right now.

is PokeIQ.

---

# The PokeIQ Assistant

As PokeIQ grows, navigating dozens of settings, pages, filters, and tools could become overwhelming.

AI should help prevent that complexity from becoming the player's problem.

Eventually, players should be able to communicate with PokeIQ naturally.

Examples:

> Only notify me about nearby Pokémon if they would improve one of my raid teams.

> I'm getting too many notifications.

> What's the best thing I can do for my account today?

> Should I use my Rare Candy on this?

> What is my weakest raid team?

> Show me Pokémon nearby that can help with my current research.

> I want to build a complete shiny Living Dex in Pokémon Violet.

> Turn off raid notifications while I'm at work.

The assistant should understand the request, use the appropriate PokeIQ systems, and when appropriate modify the relevant settings for the player.

AI should not exist simply because PokeIQ can include AI.

Its purpose should be to make an increasingly powerful application feel increasingly simple.

The player should not have to understand how PokeIQ works internally.

They should be able to describe what they want.

---

# The Live World

One of PokeIQ's largest long-term opportunities is connecting account intelligence with what is happening in the Pokémon world right now.

A future live map could display:

- Pokémon spawns
- Raids
- Max Battles
- Nests
- Relevant locations
- Research opportunities
- Other useful live-world information where reliable data is available

But the goal should not simply be to create another Pokémon map.

PokeIQ should interpret the map for the player.

A player could receive alerts such as:

> HIGH VALUE — Mamoswine nearby  
> Estimated Ground Team improvement: +5%

or:

> RESEARCH OPPORTUNITY  
> Several Generation IV Pokémon are currently clustered in this area.

or:

> COLLECTION OPPORTUNITY  
> A Pokémon missing from your Living Dex is nearby.

Notifications should be customizable and high-signal.

Players should eventually be able to define rules such as:

- Notify me about anything that improves a raid team.
- Only notify me about improvements greater than 3%.
- Notify me about Pokémon needed for active research.
- Notify me about Pokémon in selected Collections.
- Notify me about missing Pokédex entries.
- Notify me about specific species.
- Do not send low-priority notifications.

The goal is not more notifications.

The goal is better notifications.

---

# Routes and Opportunity Planning

Eventually, PokeIQ could move beyond individual map markers.

If enough live-world information is available, PokeIQ could identify useful areas or routes.

For example:

> This 40-minute route contains several Generation IV spawns, two Pokémon missing from your Living Dex, and a raid boss relevant to your Fighting team.

Instead of forcing the player to interpret dozens of markers, PokeIQ could help turn the surrounding game world into an actionable plan.

---

# Research Intelligence

Players should eventually be able to track active Pokémon GO research inside PokeIQ.

PokeIQ should understand the requirements rather than merely store the task text.

For example:

> Catch 30 Pokémon originally discovered in the Sinnoh region.

PokeIQ could identify which nearby Pokémon qualify.

Research intelligence could eventually connect with:

- Live spawns
- Nests
- Events
- Raid bosses
- Field Research
- Collections
- Player goals

This allows PokeIQ to answer:

> What can I do right now to make progress on my research?

---

# Current and Upcoming Game Intelligence

PokeIQ should understand both the present and the known future of Pokémon GO.

Eventually this may include:

- Current raid rotations
- Upcoming raid rotations
- Raid tiers
- Max Battle rotations
- Community Days
- Spotlight Hours
- Research events
- Seasonal changes
- Limited-time bonuses
- Announced Pokémon releases
- Other confirmed upcoming events

Information should distinguish clearly between:

- Confirmed
- Expected
- Predicted
- Unknown

Future information should feed directly into PokeIQ's recommendation systems.

A resource decision should not necessarily be evaluated only against what exists today.

PokeIQ should eventually be capable of saying:

> This investment would improve your account today, but waiting may provide substantially more value because of an upcoming confirmed event.

This is especially important for scarce resources such as:

- Rare Candy
- Rare Candy XL
- Elite TMs
- Stardust
- Evolution items
- Other limited resources

---

# Raid Intelligence

Raids should eventually become a unified PokeIQ experience rather than several disconnected tools.

The primary Raid experience should focus on:

- What is currently in raids
- What is coming soon
- Which raids matter to the player's account
- What team the player should use
- Whether the raid is worth spending resources on
- How much catching or investing in the boss could improve the account

The current Boss Analysis system should eventually evolve away from requiring the player to manually select any Pokémon and assign a raid tier.

The default experience should know the active raid rotation and each boss's tier automatically.

The player could simply select an active boss.

PokeIQ would already understand the encounter.

An advanced mode could remain available for:

- Historical bosses
- Hypothetical bosses
- Testing
- Future planning
- Arbitrary matchups

Future raid support should include all relevant raid tiers and special raid categories.

---

# Raid Coordination

PokeIQ may eventually explore raid hosting and remote raid coordination.

Existing raid applications demonstrate that worldwide matchmaking is extremely valuable.

PokeIQ should study where those experiences create unnecessary friction and determine whether it can improve them.

Potential areas include:

- Larger coordinated groups where Pokémon GO permits them
- Better host/guest communication
- Raid readiness
- Team recommendations before joining
- Account-specific raid value
- Lobby reliability
- Smarter matchmaking

The objective should not be to clone another raid application.

PokeIQ should identify what can be made meaningfully better.

---

# Collections and Goals

Collections should become more than folders.

Players should be able to create completely custom Collections based on whatever matters to them.

Examples could include:

- Living Dex
- Shiny Living Dex
- Hundos
- Favorite Pokémon
- Raid attackers
- Pokémon being prepared for future investment
- Personal challenges

PokeIQ should also suggest Collections based on the player's Pokémon.

Examples might include:

- Near-perfect Pokémon
- Valuable Shadows
- Pokémon worth preserving
- Potential raid investments
- Incomplete evolutionary families
- Emerging collections detected from player behavior

Collections could eventually become actionable goals.

If a Pokémon belonging to one of the player's Collections appears nearby, PokeIQ could recognize the opportunity.

Collections may eventually be shareable.

Community-created Collections could allow players to follow challenges or curated goals such as:

> Top Raid Attackers

and see their own progress automatically.

---

# Account Goals

PokeIQ should allow players to define broader goals.

Examples:

> Build a strong Ground raid team.

> Complete my Kanto Shiny Living Dex.

> Prepare for an upcoming raid boss.

> Reach six strong attackers for every type.

PokeIQ should track progress toward those goals and use them when determining recommendations.

Eventually, Goals may become the player-facing evolution of the current Projects system.

---

# Resource Intelligence

Resources should not be viewed only as numbers.

PokeIQ should understand their opportunity cost.

Eventually, the app should help answer questions such as:

> Should I spend this Rare Candy?

> Is this Pokémon worth an Elite TM?

> Should I power this Pokémon up or save the Stardust?

> How much would this investment actually improve my account?

> Is something better likely to become available soon?

Resource recommendations should consider both immediate and future value.

---

# What-If Planning

Players should eventually be able to simulate decisions before committing to them.

Examples:

> What happens to my Ground team if I evolve this Pokémon?

> What if I power this Pokémon to Level 50?

> What if I Elite TM this move?

> What if I catch the raid boss currently available?

PokeIQ could calculate the projected effect across the player's account before any resources are spent.

---

# Storage Intelligence

Managing Pokémon storage is one of the largest recurring tasks in Pokémon GO.

PokeIQ should eventually help players determine what is safe to transfer.

This should not simply be:

> Keep high IV Pokémon.

Instead, PokeIQ should understand whether a Pokémon has value because of:

- Raid performance
- PvP potential if supported
- Evolution potential
- Rarity
- Shadow status
- Collection membership
- Projects
- Research
- Trading potential
- Personal goals

The player should receive an explanation before PokeIQ recommends removing something.

---

# Importing and Official Integration

PokeIQ currently depends on player-provided collection information.

Import methods may evolve over time.

Possible methods include:

- Manual entry
- Screenshots
- Screen recordings
- CSV or structured imports where useful
- Other supported import formats

The ideal long-term solution would be official player-account integration.

PokeIQ should be architected so that its intelligence systems do not depend permanently on any single import method.

If PokeIQ eventually develops meaningful adoption, a long-term objective is to approach the company responsible for Pokémon GO with a working product and demonstrate the value of official integration.

The ideal future experience would allow authorized player data to flow directly into PokeIQ rather than requiring screenshots, recordings, or extensive manual correction.

PokeIQ should be valuable without official integration.

But it should be ready for official integration if that opportunity ever becomes possible.

---

# Beyond Pokémon GO

PokeIQ does not necessarily need to remain exclusively a Pokémon GO application forever.

The broader long-term opportunity is:

> Intelligence for Pokémon players.

Other Pokémon games could eventually exist as optional modules within PokeIQ.

For Pokémon Scarlet and Violet, for example, PokeIQ could potentially support:

- Living Dex tracking
- Shiny Living Dex tracking
- Shiny hunt tracking
- Egg counters
- Shiny egg statistics
- Species locations
- Encounter requirements
- Time-of-day requirements
- Evolution requirements
- Sandwich/hunting assistance
- Collection goals

Future Pokémon games could receive their own modules.

The core PokeIQ identity should remain consistent:

> Understand what the player wants and help them accomplish it.

Pokémon GO should remain the primary focus while the core product is being established.

Expansion should happen because it creates meaningful value, not simply because another Pokémon game exists.

---

# Platform Vision

PokeIQ currently exists as a locally hosted React application during development.

That is a development environment, not the intended final product.

Long term, PokeIQ should be an always-available application.

Mobile should become a major part of the experience because many future concepts depend on:

- Notifications
- Location awareness
- Live opportunities
- Quick decisions
- Pokémon GO being played on the same device

A web application may continue to exist alongside mobile.

The long-term platform may therefore include:

- Mobile application
- Web application
- Cloud backend
- Persistent player accounts
- Secure synchronized player data
- Background services
- Notification infrastructure
- Live-data services
- AI services

The current React application should be treated as the beginning of PokeIQ, not something that must eventually be discarded.

Architecture should evolve deliberately as the product requires it.

---

# Navigation Should Remain Simple

PokeIQ will become substantially more powerful over time.

Its navigation should not become substantially more complicated.

The current development application contains several top-level areas because features have been built independently.

Long term, related capabilities should be consolidated into player-focused hubs.

A possible future structure could resemble:

## Home

What matters to me right now?

## World

What useful things are happening around me?

## Raids

What raids are available, which matter to me, and what should I use?

## Collection

What do I own, what am I collecting, and how do I manage it?

## Goals

What am I currently working toward?

## Assistant

Ask PokeIQ anything.

Imports, settings, resources, development tools, and specialized analysis screens do not necessarily need permanent top-level navigation.

The exact navigation can change.

The principle should remain:

> PokeIQ's power should grow faster than its complexity.

---

# Monetization Philosophy

PokeIQ should respect the player.

The free application should be genuinely useful and fully functional.

Players should not install PokeIQ only to discover that its fundamental purpose is locked behind a subscription.

Core account intelligence should remain broadly accessible.

Some advanced capabilities may eventually require payment.

Potential premium capabilities could include:

- Advanced live-map functionality
- Specialized integrations
- Additional Pokémon-game modules
- Advanced automation
- Other high-cost or non-core capabilities

Where practical, a small one-time purchase is preferred over turning PokeIQ into an expensive recurring subscription.

However, some future features may create unavoidable ongoing costs.

Examples include:

- Cloud infrastructure
- AI inference
- Large-scale live-map infrastructure
- Third-party data services
- Notifications at scale

If recurring pricing ever becomes necessary for a specific capability, it should exist because that capability creates recurring costs and meaningful recurring value — not simply because subscriptions generate more revenue.

The guiding principle is:

> PokeIQ should be sustainable for its developer without becoming hostile to its players.

---

# Learning From Other Pokémon Apps

PokeIQ should actively study existing Pokémon tools.

This includes understanding:

- What players love
- What players tolerate
- What players complain about
- What requires too many steps
- What information is difficult to understand
- What limitations exist
- What could be made more personal

The objective is not:

> Copy every feature from every Pokémon app.

The objective is:

> Understand why players use those features and determine whether PokeIQ can create a better experience.

If PokeIQ eventually replaces several separate applications for some players, it should happen because those capabilities work better when connected by one personalized intelligence system.

# Product Principles

## 1. Decisions Over Data

Information matters most when PokeIQ can explain what the player should do with it.

## 2. Personalization Over Generic Rankings

The best Pokémon in theory is not always the best decision for a specific account.

## 3. Explain Recommendations

Players should understand why PokeIQ is recommending something.

## 4. Respect Player Resources

Rare resources represent significant player time and should never be treated casually.

## 5. Respect Player Attention

Notifications should be valuable enough that players want to keep them enabled.

## 6. Reduce Complexity

Adding features should not require players to memorize increasingly complicated navigation.

## 7. Build for the Future Without Depending on It

PokeIQ can prepare for live data, official integrations, AI, and other future capabilities without requiring them for the current product to succeed.

## 8. Free Should Actually Mean Useful

Premium features should expand PokeIQ rather than rescue an intentionally crippled free experience.

## 9. Do Not Build Features Just Because We Can

Every major feature should have a clear player problem it solves.

## 10. The Player Owns the Goal

PokeIQ provides intelligence.

The player decides what matters.

Efficiency, collecting, shinies, raids, research, personal favorites, and unusual goals can all be legitimate priorities.

# The Long-Term Experience

The ultimate version of PokeIQ might understand:

- The player's entire Pokémon collection
- Their strongest teams
- Their weakest teams
- Their resources
- Their active Projects
- Their personal Collections
- Their current research
- Their long-term goals
- Their local opportunities
- Current raids
- Upcoming events
- Future known releases
- Historical availability
- Their notification preferences
- Other Pokémon games they play

From that information, PokeIQ could continuously identify useful opportunities.

A player might open PokeIQ and see:

> Good morning.

> Your biggest opportunity today is the current raid rotation. One of the available bosses would improve your Ice team from 71 to 78.

> You also have an active research task requiring Sinnoh Pokémon. There is currently a useful spawn cluster approximately ten minutes away.

> You have enough Rare Candy to finish your current Project, but I recommend saving it. A confirmed raid boss arriving next week would provide significantly more account value.

> Three Pokémon nearby match Collections you're currently working on.

And the player could simply respond:

> I only have about an hour. What should I do?

PokeIQ would understand the context and build an answer around that player.

That is the destination.

---

# The North Star

PokeIQ should become the Pokémon assistant that understands the difference between:

> What is good?

and:

> What is good for me?

Every system — Collection, Raids, Resources, Goals, Research, Live Map, Events, AI, and future Pokémon games — should ultimately contribute to answering that second question.

PokeIQ does not need to know everything.

It needs to know enough to help the player make the next decision better.
